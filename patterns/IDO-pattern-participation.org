** IDO-pattern-participation ontology (pattern:IDO-pattern-participation pattern:IDO-pattern-participation/0.0)
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       IDO-pattern-participation-ontology-declaration
:resourcedefs: yes
:END:
 - owl:versionInfo :: 0.0 start of IDO-pattern-participation
 - dcterms:modified ::  {{{time("%Y-%m-%d")}}}^^xsd:date
 - dcterms:description :: This ontology is an IDO modelling pattern: an activity has two objects participating in it in distinct roles -- an active participant (the one acting) and a passive participant (the one acted upon). The anchor is the activity, not the object, and no quality, datum, unit or literal appears. IDO expresses participant roles as a small fixed set of sub-relations, not as role individuals, so a role cannot itself be dated, qualified or given a provenance; and the set does not cover all thematic roles.
 - Import :: pattern:pattern-vocabulary
 - Import :: lis-ont:core

*** Relationship to IDO-pattern-idef0                           :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-participation-relationship-idef0
:END:

[[file:IDO-pattern-idef0.org][patterns/IDO-pattern-idef0.org]] uses a more detailed role vocabulary: it
keeps this pattern's anchor, assertion direction and cardinality
treatment, and replaces the active/passive distinction with IDEF0's
four roles (Input, Control, Output, Mechanism), declared as
sub-properties of the relations used here.

Choose that pattern when the domain is a systems-engineering workflow
and the four roles are clear; choose this one when they are not. Every
idef0 application reduces to a participation application; the reverse
does not hold, so the choice is made at authoring time. That file's
/Relationship to IDO-pattern-participation/ block gives the
sub-property axioms in full.

*** How to apply this pattern                                   :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-participation-howto
:END:

The generic procedure is in [[file:pattern-framework.org::*Applying a pattern: the generic procedure][Applying a pattern: the generic
procedure]]. Specific to /this/ pattern:

- *The activity is the anchor.* Both participation legs are asserted
  on ~var:TheActivity~, in IDO's asserted direction
  (~lis:hasParticipant~ has ~Domain lis:Activity~, ~Range
  lis:Object~). Writing the inverse (~lis:participantIn~) instead is
  logically equivalent but scatters an activity's participants over
  several headings.
- *Roles are sub-relations.* IDO 4.2 provides
  ~lis:hasActiveParticipant~, ~lis:hasPassiveParticipant~ and
  ~lis:hasPrimaryParticipant~ under ~lis:hasParticipant~ (each with an
  inverse). There is no role class and no participation individual:
  the role is given by the choice of property. If a role outside this
  set is needed, that is a domain-ontology extension (a further
  sub-property).
- ~lis:hasPrimaryParticipant~ is *not* part of the minimal pattern.
  IDO reserves it for the participant whose disposition or role the
  activity essentially realizes -- an identity claim about the
  activity, stronger than "the one acting". Add it at apply time when
  that stronger claim holds; do not treat it as a synonym for the
  active participant.
- *The activity kind is the one bind-first slot.*
  ~var:ActivityKind~ is ~BIND_OR_MINT~: established once per ontology
  (Pumping, Umbrella assembly) and shared by every later
  application. The two participant classes are ~BIND~. All three
  individuals are fresh per application.
- *Cardinality: handled concretely, not generally.* See below.

*** Cardinality: what this pattern commits to                    :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-participation-cardinality
:END:

*Handled:* exactly two participation legs, in two fixed, distinct
roles. That is a fixed arity, so no expansion machinery is needed --
the pattern is a two-slot template and each slot's role is given by
the property it hangs from.

*Not handled:* an activity with /n/ passive participants (a bolting
activity joining four plates). Apply the pattern again to the same
~var:TheActivity~ -- bind the activity, mint only the participant.
This works because the legs are independent: nothing has to be
correlated across them. It relies on the anchor slot being
~MINT_OR_BIND~ rather than ~MINT~, which is a deliberate choice here
and should be made consciously in any future pattern with a repeatable
leg.

*** Required constants                                          :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-participation-constants
:END:

All from ~lis-ont:core~ (IDO), verified against IDO 4.2:

- ~lis:Activity~ -- domain of the participation relations.
- ~lis:Object~ -- their range; ancestor of both participant classes.
- ~lis:hasParticipant~ (~Domain lis:Activity~, ~Range lis:Object~,
  inverse ~lis:participantIn~), carrying three property chains,
  including ~lis:hasActivityPart o lis:hasParticipant~ -- so a
  participant in a sub-activity is a participant in the whole.
- ~lis:hasActiveParticipant~ and ~lis:hasPassiveParticipant~, both
  nested under ~lis:hasParticipant~.

Nesting matters here: the two role relations have no ~Domain~/~Range~
of their own and inherit both from ~lis:hasParticipant~. Declared at
top level in a target ontology they would be unconstrained.

*** Worked example: assembling Ursula's umbrella                 :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-participation-example
:END:

"In the assembly of Ursula's umbrella, the handle is fitted to the
shaft" -- the shaft is what is acted upon, the handle is what acts.

#+caption: Substitution of pattern placeholders for the umbrella-assembly example
| pattern placeholder          | concrete resource                    |
|------------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| var:ActivityKind             | umb:umbrella-assembly                |
| var:ActiveParticipantClass   | umb:handle                           |
| var:PassiveParticipantClass  | umb:shaft                            |
| var:TheActivity              | umb:ursula-s-umbrella-assembly       |
| var:TheActiveParticipant     | umb:X_02B7PM7KB (Ursula's handle)    |
| var:ThePassiveParticipant    | umb:X_02B7FWK6B (Ursula's shaft)     |

Reading the result: one activity individual of kind
~umb:umbrella-assembly~, with two asserted legs distinguishing what
acts from what is acted upon. Both participants are *bound*, not
minted -- they already exist in the target ontology.

*Second application, same activity.* To add the canopy as a further
passive participant, bind ~umb:ursula-s-umbrella-assembly~ (the
~MINT_OR_BIND~ anchor) and write only the new leg. See /Cardinality/
above.
