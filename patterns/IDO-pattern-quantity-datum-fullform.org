*** How to apply this pattern                                   :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-quantity-datum-fullform-howto
:END:

The generic procedure is in [[file:pattern-framework.org::*Applying a pattern: the generic procedure][Applying a pattern: the generic
procedure]]. Specific to /this/ pattern:

- The placeholder classes are ~var:DomainClass~ (the kind of object
  being described), ~var:DomainQuantityKind~ (the quality/quantity kind
  of interest, typically from QUDT), and
  ~var:DomainScalarQuantityDatumType~ -- the datum kind, usually
  ~lis:ScalarQuantityDatum~ itself or a generic subtype such as
  "measured"/"nominal". Do *not* mint a per-use-case datum type.
- The placeholder individuals are ~var:TheDomainThing~,
  ~var:TheDomainQuantity~, ~var:TheDomainScalarQuantityDatum~ and
  ~var:TheUnitOfMeasure~; the two middle ones are always freshly
  minted, as they exist only to carry the quantification.
- The ~Facts~ rows linking object, quality and datum
  (~hasPhysicalQuantity~, ~qualityQuantifiedAs~, ~quantifiesQuality~,
  ~datumUOM~, ~datumValue~) are the pattern proper; keep them as
  shown.
- The literal to supply is the ~lis:datumValue~ magnitude, e.g.
  ~"0.4"^^xsd:decimal~.

*** Worked example: thickness of a piece of fabric              :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-quantity-datum-fullform-example
:END:

A concrete instantiation modelling "this fabric is 0.4 mm thick".
The substitutions are:

#+caption: Substitution of pattern placeholders for the fabric-thickness example
| pattern placeholder               | concrete resource          |
|-----------------------------------+----------------------------|
| var:DomainClass                   | ex:Fabric                  |
| var:DomainQuantityKind            | quantitykind:Thickness     |
| var:DomainScalarQuantityDatumType | ex:ThicknessDatum          |
| var:TheDomainThing                | ex:theFabric               |
| var:TheDomainQuantity             | ex:theFabricThickness      |
| var:TheDomainScalarQuantityDatum  | ex:theFabricThicknessDatum |
| var:TheUnitOfMeasure              | unit:MilliM                |
| lis:datumValue literal            | "0.4"^^xsd:decimal         |

Reading the result: ~ex:theFabric~ (an ~ex:Fabric~) has physical
quantity ~ex:theFabricThickness~ (an ~ex:Thickness~), which is
quantified as ~ex:theFabricThicknessDatum~ (an ~ex:ThicknessDatum~)
carrying the value ~0.4~ with unit ~unit:MilliM~ (millimetre).

*** Relationship to the shortcut patterns                       :nodeclare:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IDO-pattern-quantity-datum-fullform-siblings
:END:

Siblings: [[file:IDO-pattern-quantity-shortcut-object.org][IDO-pattern-quantity-shortcut-object]] (pattern 2) and
[[file:IDO-pattern-quantity-shortcut-data.org][IDO-pattern-quantity-shortcut-data]] (pattern 3). This is the most
detailed of the three; the other two omit part of it.

- *Pattern 2* drops the quality individual ~var:TheDomainQuantity~ and
  its kind ~var:DomainQuantityKind~, linking object to datum directly
  by a minted sub-property of ~lis:hasQualityQuantifiedAs~ whose name
  encodes the quality kind.
- *Pattern 3* drops the quality, the datum /and/ the unit individual,
  linking object to a bare literal by a minted sub-property of
  ~lis:qualityQuantityValue~ whose name encodes both quality and unit.

IDO asserts ~lis:hasQualityQuantifiedAs SubPropertyChain:
lis:hasQuality o lis:qualityQuantifiedAs~, so an application of this
pattern entails the pattern-2 shortcut link at the level of the IDO
super-property, given the import. No chain axiom is asserted here or
in the siblings on the minted shortcut properties; see pattern 2.

The omissions go one way only: recovering a dropped individual would
mean generating one, which OWL 2 DL cannot do. Choose this pattern
whenever the quality or the datum may later need to be referred to.
